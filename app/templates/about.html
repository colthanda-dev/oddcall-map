How this map works

Every pin is a report, not a verdict. The map records who said what, when, and how it got here. It never says a report is true or false.

Three dates, kept apart

When it happened. When the witness first told anyone. When it hit the public record. Most databases collapse these into one date and lose the only thing that lets you judge a story. We keep all three, and a blank stays blank.

Three layers, never mixed

Documented history. Folklore with named sources. Anomalous reports with full provenance. A report page tells you which layer it belongs to by its source and its credibility tier.

Credibility tiers

Set by a moderator, never by votes. The tier describes provenance, not truth.

TierMeans
Field verifiedA moderator interviewed the witness or visited the site; all three dates recorded.
Witness, directFirst-hand account through the hotline or the form, with usable contact.
Witness, anonymousFirst-hand account, no contact or contact declined.
Public record, unverifiedPulled from a public database or forum. Excerpt shown; the full account stays at its source.
SecondhandSomeone reporting what another person saw.
DisputedFlagged by a moderator with a stated reason.

What gets published, and what does not

Nothing goes up without a human click. The public pin is offset 300 to 800 m from the reported point and never sits on a home. Witness contact never leaves the moderator screen. We do not name private individuals as hoaxers or perpetrators, do not publish reports that appear to come from or involve a minor, and reject promotional content.

Takedowns and corrections

Every report page has a removal link. We act within 48 hours, no questions. Property owners can ask that their land be removed from the map.
